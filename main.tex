\title{From one to zero: Calibrated Hardware In-memory Lane Learning
  with Zero-Ended Noise}

\author{M. Alexander Nugent}
\affiliation{Knowm Inc.}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We present CHILL ZEN---Calibrated Hardware In-memory Lane Learning
with Zero-Ended Noise---a generative architecture for probabilistic
in-memory hardware. Each weight is a differential memristor pair, and
a read returns the weight plus noise whose width tracks the weight's
own uncertainty. The sampling temperature is an argument of the read
instruction, over a range that reaches zero, so generation is one hot
autoregressive pass over a discrete address code followed by one
zero-temperature reconciliation sweep, with no mixing phase. A lane
read pools thousands of pairs, and pooling quenches the read's own
noise: at the sense floor the devices deliver about a third of the
noise the draw needs. The rest comes from the comparator that resolves
the read. Sweeping its input-referred noise locates the operating
point, shows that the comparator's share of the noise rather than the
read voltage sets the temperature, and fixes a design requirement on
the comparator. On grayscale Fashion-MNIST a two-level stack reaches
judge parity with real images on its reconstruction arm and scores
above the real bar end-to-end, in about 131 sequential read steps per
image. Binarized and scored under the evaluation protocol of the
denoising thermodynamic model of Jelin\v{c}i\v{c} \emph{et al.}, the
same renders give FID 18.7 against 24.9 for their deepest chain, at a
modeled $1.9\times10^{-11}$\,J per image against their
$1.57\times10^{-8}$\,J; trained directly on the binarized data, the
architecture scores FID 11.1.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The energy consumption of generative AI has become a first-order
constraint on its deployment, and it has prompted a search for
computers in which sampling is performed by physics rather than
simulated over it \cite{conte2019thermodynamic}. Probabilistic bits
made from stochastic nanomagnets and related devices have run
optimization and learning workloads at very low energy
\cite{sutton2017intrinsic,borders2019integer,niazi2024training}, and
Jelin\v{c}i\v{c} \emph{et al.}\ \cite{jelincic2025dtm} recently
proposed a complete generative system---model class, architecture,
and energy analysis---on all-transistor probabilistic circuits.

That work states the central obstacle cleanly: the
mixing--expressivity tradeoff. As a monolithic energy-based model
(EBM) fits multimodal data better, energy barriers grow between the
modes, and the mixing time of any locally informed sampler grows with
them. Their answer is the Denoising Thermodynamic Model (DTM), a chain
of $T = 2$--$8$ EBMs that reverses a noising process in the manner of
diffusion models \cite{sohldickstein2015deep,ho2020ddpm} so that each
step stays easy to mix, and the Denoising Thermodynamic Computer
Architecture (DTCA), which implements each step on sparse bipartite
Boltzmann machines whose nodes are subthreshold-transistor
random-number generators (RNGs) with a measured decorrelation time
$\tau_0 \approx 100$\,ns \cite{jelincic2025dtm,freitas2025taming}.

This paper describes a different answer to the same constraint: make
the sampling temperature an argument of the read instruction, over a
range that reaches zero, so that generation is one hot pass over the
code followed by one cold sweep rather than a mixing chain. We call the system CHILL
ZEN, for Calibrated Hardware In-memory Lane Learning with Zero-Ended
Noise. Both designs are instances of thermodynamic computing; the
thermal constant has been in the substrate's name since 2014
\cite{nugent2014cortical,nugent2014ahah}.

The substrate supplies its randomness from the stored weights
themselves: a read returns the weight plus a noise whose width tracks
the weight's own uncertainty. The paper's most consequential finding
concerns where that noise is not enough. A lane read pools thousands
of weight pairs, and pooling quenches the read's own noise in the same
proportion that it sharpens the answer. At the sense floor the devices
deliver about a third of the noise a generative draw needs. The rest
has to be supplied by the comparator that resolves each read, which
makes the comparator's input-referred noise the draw's temperature
knob and places a design requirement on it that we state and measure.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item CHILL ZEN, a generative model class for lane hardware, defined
  by its code, its paired generator and repair banks, and its
  one-pass, one-sweep procedure (Sec.~\ref{sec:model});
\item the two-level result on grayscale Fashion-MNIST
  \cite{xiao2017fashion}, with the residual error localized to one
  component, the label$\to$code draw (Secs.~\ref{sec:headline} and
  \ref{sec:gap});
\item the comparator-noise result: pooled reads quench their own
  noise, the comparator supplies the draw temperature, and a sweep of
  its noise setting fixes the operating point and the design
  requirement (Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator});
\item a comparison with the DTM/DTCA as design points, a modeled
  energy accounting, and a head-to-head on binarized Fashion-MNIST
  scored with the released evaluation code of
  Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm} (Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison}).
\end{enumerate}
Every result is emulated lane computation: device models with the
read-noise physics of Sec.~\ref{sec:substrate}, every read and adapt
issued as a lane instruction, and decode, render, and judging in
host-side floating point. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} gives the full
accounting.

\section{The substrate}\label{sec:substrate}

\subsection{The kT-bit and its read noise}\label{sec:ktbit}

The storage and sampling element is a differential pair of metastable
memristors read through a voltage divider. With conductances $G_a$
and $G_b$ and read voltage $V$, the divider reports
\begin{equation}
V_y = V\,\frac{G_a - G_b}{G_a + G_b}, \qquad
y = \frac{V_y}{V} \in [-1, 1],
\label{eq:divider}
\end{equation}
and the pair carries two state variables,
\begin{equation}
w = \frac{G_a - G_b}{G_a + G_b}, \qquad m = G_a + G_b .
\label{eq:wm}
\end{equation}
The weight $w$ is the balance of the pair and the magnitude $m$ is its
total. The magnitude is the weight's inertia: a feedback pulse moves
$w$ in proportion to $1/m$, so the effective learning rate falls on
the Robbins--Monro $1/n$ schedule with no schedule stored anywhere.
It is also a count of evidence. Forward feedback increments one
conductance by a quantum, which is the conjugate update of a Beta
distribution, so the pair holds a Beta posterior with mean and
concentration
\begin{equation}
\mu = \frac{G_a}{G_a + G_b}, \qquad \kappa = G_a + G_b, \qquad
\sigma^2 = \frac{\mu(1-\mu)}{\kappa + 1}.
\label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}

A read returns the stored weight plus noise. The noise has a thermal
term and a flicker term from the devices, and a third term from the
comparator that resolves the read, summed in quadrature:
\begin{align}
\sigma_{\mathrm{thermal}} &\propto \frac{\sqrt{\kB T}}{V\sqrt{m}},
\qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{flicker}} \propto \frac{1 - w^2}{\sqrt{m}},
\nonumber \\
\sigma_{\mathrm{comparator}} &= \frac{v_n}{V},
\nonumber \\
\sigma_y &= \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{thermal}}^2 +
                  \sigma_{\mathrm{flicker}}^2 +
                  \sigma_{\mathrm{comparator}}^2}.
\label{eq:noise}
\end{align}
The thermal term rides as $1/V$, so the read voltage is a
per-instruction temperature dial, and at full read voltage the device
terms sit at the flicker floor (Fig.~\ref{fig:noise}). We call such a
read cold and treat it as $T = 0$, because the winning lane of a
winner-take-all group clears its rivals by about 46 times that floor
(Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}). Both device terms fall as $1/\sqrt{m}$,
and the flicker term carries $(1 - w^2)$, so a read's scatter narrows
the two ways the posterior of Eq.~(\ref{eq:beta}) narrows: with
accumulated evidence, and toward the certain ends. The match is in
the form and not in the exponents; a hot read is a sample whose width
tracks the weight's own uncertainty, not a draw from the posterior
itself. This is the C in CHILL: the randomness is calibrated, not
merely present. The sign of one subthreshold read is a writable coin,
a bit with $P(+) = \Phi(w/\sigma_y)$ whose bias is the stored weight.

The comparator term is different in kind. It carries no dependence on
$w$ or $m$, only on the comparator's input-referred noise $v_n$
referred to the read voltage. That noise is kT/C noise on the
regeneration nodes plus the preamplifier's own thermal noise
\cite{razavi2015strongarm}. It is a settable circuit parameter
rather than a fixed one: it rises when the preamplifier bias is
starved, the latch is strobed earlier, or a deliberate noise source is
added at the input. It falls only at a cost in area and power,
down to a floor set by the comparator's design class. The comparator's
input offset is a separate quantity, a static per-lane bias set by
transistor matching \cite{pelgrom1989matching}; we treat it as absorbed
by learning, as a network trained against any fixed per-lane term
absorbs it.

Why the comparator term matters is a consequence of pooling, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} measures it. Because both device terms fall
as $1/\sqrt{m}$, and a lane read pools the magnitudes of every pair it
selects, a well-taught pool is quiet twice over: the wire averages
more evidence, and the same conductance that carries the answer damps
the read's own noise. The context that lets a bank speak with
confidence is the context that quiets the bath it would need to sample
from. At the sense floor the lanes of the deployed system deliver a
read noise of about 0.02--0.04 on their own, and the draw needs 0.1
or more. The comparator supplies the difference, and the temperature
schedule of a CHILL ZEN generation is a bias schedule on that one
shared circuit: hot reads starve the preamplifier, cold reads run it at
full bias.

Using device variability as the sampler rather than designing it out
is established. Dalgaty \emph{et al.}\ \cite{dalgaty2021insitu} run
Markov chain Monte Carlo in a 16{,}384-device array by treating the
cycle-to-cycle variability of a memristive write as the proposal
distribution, and Cai \emph{et al.}\ \cite{cai2020hopfield} use
intrinsic device noise as the annealing source in a memristor Hopfield
network. What Eq.~(\ref{eq:noise}) adds is that the noise is a
property of the instruction and of one shared peripheral circuit, not
of the device alone: the same weight can be read hot or cold, and the
range reaches zero.

\subsection{The neural lane}\label{sec:lane}

A neural lane is an array of unit-crossbar pairs pooled on a shared
2-1 divider. By Kirchhoff's laws the selected pairs report
\begin{equation}
y = \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} (G_{a,i} - G_{b,i})}
         {\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} (G_{a,i} + G_{b,i})}
  = \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} m_i w_i}
         {\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} m_i},
\label{eq:pool}
\end{equation}
the magnitude-weighted, which is to say inverse-variance-weighted,
average of the selected weights. The pooling that Bayesian evidence
combination asks for is performed by the wire. Selection is an
Activation Address Tuple (AAT): an ordered tuple of integers, one per
address space, and the tuple is the wiring pattern pin for pin. One
consequence shapes the model class: a space's influence on a read is
its share of the pool's total magnitude, roughly $1/K$ when $K$
spaces are active, so an address that carries no measured value takes
influence from the ones that do, and an address that must condition
the read has to be replicated across several spaces.

Table~\ref{tab:isa} lists the kT-RAM instruction set. Instruction
codes are two letters: the first is the drive direction (F forward, R
reverse), the second the operation. Forward feedback accumulates
magnitude and reverse feedback depletes it; every working routine
pairs a forward operation with a reverse one.

\subsection{WTA Groups and the lane routines}\label{sec:routines}

Lanes are organized into winner-take-all (WTA) Groups, one lane per
symbol of an address space, and three routines cover everything in
this paper; each is described in depth in an online chapter series
\cite{knowm2026blog}. In the \emph{supervised three-case rule}, all
lanes of a group are read with \texttt{FF}; the labeled lane receives
\texttt{RH}; a wrong lane that fired receives \texttt{RL} (hard) or
nothing beyond the paired reverse read (soft); a correctly silent lane
receives \texttt{RF}. Cold argmax readout of a group taught this way
is a classifier at parity with logistic regression on the identical
encoding (Iris: 0.962 against 0.961 over twenty splits). Hard feedback
carves a margin; soft feedback keeps every answer the data supports,
so a soft bank read hot draws from the distribution the data left in
its weights, and the chain rule runs as sequential lane reads: clamp
what is known, read one space, commit, continue. \emph{RankCut
readout} reports the above-threshold lanes of a group in rank order
from a shared falling reference and one comparator per lane, with no
per-lane analog-to-digital converter. The \emph{WTA competitive rule}
drops the label and targets the read winner, with exclusion and
recruitment in the spirit of conscience mechanisms
\cite{desieno1988conscience}, so a group learns a codebook with no
teacher.

\subsection{The emulator}\label{sec:emulator}

All results are emulated lane computation on
\texttt{ktram-neural-core} \cite{knowm2026emulator}: device models
with the read-noise physics above, a torch runtime bit-exact against
the reference core, and every read and adapt issued as a lane
instruction. The physical memristors and crossbars exist as fabricated
and commercially sold hardware \cite{knowm2026datasheet}; the
lane-scale system does not yet. The emulator's device noise gain
(0.02 at its reference operating point) is pinned throughout and never
raised to drive a hot read. A hot read is driven by the read voltage
and by the comparator term of Eq.~(\ref{eq:noise}), both physical
operating points.

\section{The model class}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{The code}\label{sec:code}

Images are represented in a per-position dropout additive-quantization
(dropout-AQ) code: $k$ books of $S$ atoms at keep probability $p$, in
the lineage of vector and additive quantization
\cite{gray1984vq,jegou2011product,babenko2014additive} and of
generation over discrete codes
\cite{vandenoord2017vqvae,chang2022maskgit}. A code is a point in
$\A_S^k$ ($k$ address spaces of $S$ symbols each), mirroring the
codeword space $\mathbb{F}_q^n$ of coding theory. The training
objective marginalizes Bernoulli address deletion,
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}_z \Bigl\| x - b - \sum_j z_j a_j \Bigr\|^2
&= \Bigl\| x - b - p \sum_j a_j \Bigr\|^2 \nonumber \\
&\quad + p(1-p) \sum_j \| a_j \|^2,
\label{eq:dropoutaq}
\end{align}
with $z_j \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$, and decoding, including of
partial states, is the fixed linear map
\begin{equation}
\hat{x} = b + p \sum_{j \in \mathrm{present}} a_j .
\label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
The keep probability is a coherence-versus-fidelity dial: $p = 1$
recovers plain additive quantization \cite{babenko2014additive}, with
the best floor and brittle partial states, and $p < 1$ trades floor
for cross-address redundancy. The founding property of the code is
that partial codes are in-distribution by construction: a half-filled
address state decodes to a coherent soft image, which is what makes
hole-filling a learnable lane task.

Stripped of the hardware, the model class is generation over a linear
additive-quantization codebook with masked-context training and a
parallel reconciliation sweep, which places it beside MaskGIT
\cite{chang2022maskgit} and the VQ-VAE lineage
\cite{vandenoord2017vqvae}. The decode is a fixed
linear map rather than a learned network, which is what makes partial
states meaningful and the energy accounting of Sec.~\ref{sec:energy}
possible. And each level pairs a soft bank that draws with a hard
bank that reconciles, both taught by local lane instructions, where a
masked transformer uses one network and a confidence schedule.

\subsection{Paired banks and the generation procedure}\label{sec:banks}

Each level of a CHILL ZEN stack pairs a soft generator bank G, read
hot, with a hard repair bank R, taught on a corrupted pool and applied
cold in one full-commit parallel sweep. The two-level system evaluated here has a
\emph{backbone} (level 1: a global code over the whole canvas, with
banks G1 and R1) and a \emph{patch level} (level 2: a residual code on
a grid of patch slots, with bank G2 and a cross-level joint repair
bank). The \emph{render} is the backbone decode plus the patch decode.
Generation runs as follows (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item Clamp the label, replicated across six address spaces.
\item Backbone draw: an autoregressive hot read over the 128 global
  books at the operating point of Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator},
  $(V, v_n) = (10\,\mathrm{mV}, 1\,\mathrm{mV})$, each read sampling
  $p(z_m \mid z_{<m}, \ell)$ through the read noise, followed by one
  cold R1 sweep.
\item Patch level: one parallel hot G2 read, at the same operating
  point, over all 49 residual patch slots under the held global code.
\item One cold cross-level joint sweep reconciles both levels: all
  226 addresses are targets, and the sweep may flip globals bottom-up
  and patches top-down in the same parallel read.
\item Decode and render (host floating point).
\end{enumerate}

The sequential step count is 128 autoregressive reads plus three
parallel sweeps, about 131 sequential read steps per image. The
temperature schedule is two-valued and per-instruction, stated in the
comparator's noise setting at a shared read voltage: $v_n = 1$\,mV for
draws and $v_n \to 0$ for verdicts, inside one image.

\section{Training and results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Setup and judge}\label{sec:setup}

The benchmark is grayscale Fashion-MNIST \cite{xiao2017fashion},
split 68\,000 train and 2\,000 evaluation. The judge
reports four numbers: \emph{critic}, the label-agreement rate of a
frozen pixel-space multilayer perceptron
($784\to256\to\mathrm{ReLU}\to10$) on the generated images, where the
real-reference row is the bar and parity with it is the target;
\emph{div}, within-class sample diversity (fraction of pixel pairs
differing by more than $1/16$); \emph{tone}, the spread of per-image
foreground mean level within class; and \emph{std-ratio}, foreground
contrast relative to real, with an alarm margin of 1.10. No FID appears
in this section; the FID comparison with Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm}
is made in Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead}, on binarized renders, under that
work's own protocol.

Teaching runs entirely by lane instructions and no gradients are
computed anywhere. Soft G banks teach on Bernoulli-masked codes with
the label always held; hard R banks teach on realistically corrupted
pools (marginal corruption plus hot G fills); the joint bank teaches
on two-level corrupted states. Each bank is probed per epoch and the
best-probe snapshot is kept. Two choices in that recipe---holding the
label rather than masking it, and committing a single cold dose rather
than several---follow the pool-share argument of Sec.~\ref{sec:lane},
and were confirmed by ablation on a per-patch predecessor of this
pipeline. Those runs are not reproducible here and no number from them
enters this paper.

\subsection{The two-level result}\label{sec:headline}

The deployed system is a 904-bit code plus the clamped label: a
$128\times16$ global backbone at keep-$p$ 0.5 (512 bits) and a
$2\times16$ residual patch code on a $7\times7$ grid of
$4\times4$-pixel slots (392 bits). Table~\ref{tab:headline} gives the
verdict as mean $\pm$ seed spread (max $-$ min) over three fresh seeds
at $n = 1000$; the spread is the evaluation noise at this $n$. The
reconstruction arm replaces the draw with the code of a real image and
runs every other component as deployed. Its critic matches the real
bar within seed spread, so everything after the draw adds no error
this judge can see. The end-to-end arm, drawn from the label alone,
scores above both. A score above the real bar is agreement with a
fixed classifier, not evidence that the draws are better images; it
says the drawn images are at least as label-consistent as real
Fashion-MNIST on this judge.

The generated batch shows no memorization signature: over 5120 drawn

training set overlap those of held-out real images ($L_2$ first

\subsection{The comparator sets the draw temperature}\label{sec:comparator}

Every hot read in this paper runs at a modeled read voltage of
$V = 10$\,mV, the sense floor of the energy model of
Sec.~\ref{sec:energy}, with the pulse width settling-limited,
$t_b = \max(2\,\mathrm{ns}, 5RC)$. At that floor the lanes alone
deliver a device read noise of 0.02--0.04 across the backbone chain,
nearly flat in chain position because the settling time and the pooled
conductance move together, with flicker 5--25\% of the variance. The
pooled magnitude $G_\Sigma$ that Eq.~(\ref{eq:pool}) reads through is
also the denominator of the thermal term, so the devices cannot be made
to supply more: the sense floor caps what a read's own physics can
deliver, at about a third of the noise the draw turns out to need. The
comparator term $v_n / V$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:noise})---flat in $y$ and
$m$ and settable by circuit design (Table~\ref{tab:comparator})---can be raised.

\emph{The sweep.} We swept $v_n / V$ at $V = 10$\,mV from 0 (lanes
only) to 0.75 on the grayscale end-to-end draw and on the
binary-trained two-level draw of Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead}, holding
every other component as deployed. Figure~\ref{fig:comparatorsweep}
shows the result: the lanes alone fall short in both stacks, each
improves as the comparator's noise is raised, and each has an interior
optimum, at $v_n / V = 0.10$ for grayscale and $0.2$--$0.3$ for
binary. The figure also marks a control, the same draw with the
emulator's device noise gain raised by hand; no circuit produces that
noise, so the control is a reference and not an operating point.

Three things the figure does not show. At the optima the comparator
supplies 88--97\% of the read's variance in the grayscale draw and
99--100\% in the binary one. A twin of the $v_n / V = 0.10$ point read
at $V = 50$\,mV, which quiets the lanes' thermal term five-fold,
matches the 10\,mV point on every judge (grayscale critic 0.9030
against 0.9043; binary FID 12.39 against 12.99): the comparator's
share sets the temperature, not the read voltage. And the flat term is
the right shape. The device noise falls as $1/\sqrt{m}$, so a draw
run on device noise alone is loudest on the first reads of the chain,
where the pool is small, and quietest on the last; the comparator term
puts the same noise at every chain position. Section~\ref{sec:gap}
measures why that matters: the chain's earliest reads are the ones
that starve.

\emph{The design requirement.} No comparator class reaches these
operating points on its own. A raw dynamic latch at a 10\,mV read
reaches $v_n / V \approx 0.2$ from its own kT/C and preamplifier
noise, so the grayscale optimum at 0.10 sits inside its range with the
preamplifier partly starved. The binary optimum at 0.2--0.3 sits at or
above it and needs either that latch starved further or a dedicated
noise source at the input. Both are ordinary circuits, but neither is
a free by-product of a comparator designed to be quiet. The
requirement CHILL ZEN places on its comparator is therefore stated
plainly: input-referred noise settable per instruction, from near
zero for verdicts to about 0.1--0.3 of the read voltage for draws,
with offset either cancelled or small enough for learning to absorb.
The cold reads run the same circuit at full bias, where the winning
lane's margin clears the residual noise by a factor of about 46.

Two caveats travel with the model. The flicker term's extrapolation
from a 1\,$\mu$s calibration to nanosecond pulses assumes the $1/f$
band extends that far; the term is 10--25\% of the variance at the
floor and does not change the conclusions above. And when the
comparator supplies most of a read's noise, the thermodynamic identity
of Sec.~\ref{sec:energy} describes only the lane's share; the
comparator's energy is counted separately there.

\subsection{Where the remaining error is}\label{sec:gap}

The generation stack factors as draw $\to$ repair $\to$ decoration
$\to$ joint sweep $\to$ render, and the reconstruction arm of
Table~\ref{tab:headline} replaces exactly one component, the draw,
with ground truth. Its parity with the real bar localizes the residual
error, conditional on the codec, to the label$\to$code draw. The control
draw of Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} isolates the same bank from the
other direction: it scores $0.8317 \pm 0.0130$, and that entire gap
belongs to the draw. The judge is silent about the codec itself: the
reconstruction arm passes through it too, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} finds that the codec alone accounts for 12.3
FID points the critic scores as zero error. The two measurements bound
different components, and neither alone bounds the system.

% Draw analysis: 10_draw_analysis.py; schedules: 20_draw_ablations.py;
% prefix reteach: 09_prefix_schedule.py.
The control draw's failure has a measured signature. Its per-book
symbol marginals deviate from the training marginals by 0.20 total
variation against a 0.05 real-sampling bar, worst in the earliest
books, where the over-picked symbols are near-zero-contrast atoms: the
chain collapses its first draws onto flat modal atoms and decodes them
too contrasty (foreground contrast 1.25 times real, against 1.11 on
the reconstruction arm). Re-encode self-consistency of the drawn codes
exceeds that of real codes, so the failure is distributional, not
disobedience to the codec, and reteaching the draw bank on its exact
run-time context schedule moved the critic by $-0.004$, at the noise
floor. Two noise schedules at the emulator's own scale test the
diagnosis. A linear schedule falling across the chain moves tone,
contrast, and diversity onto the reconstruction arm's values at a small
critic cost (0.8253), and a flat noise at the control's median is a
wash on the critic (0.8417) and costs diversity. Neither closes the
gap, because both stay at the lanes' own scale. The operating point of
Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} supplies a flat read about 3.5 times hotter,
and that is what moves tone and contrast onto the reconstruction arm's
values and lifts the critic above it (Table~\ref{tab:headline}). The
contrast alarm is not fully closed: end-to-end std-ratio is 1.15
against a 1.10 margin.

\subsection{Design studies}\label{sec:studies}

Two scaling studies shaped the deployed code, both measured on the
pipeline this paper describes. They are what these experiments showed,
not general results.

\emph{(a) Capacity and alphabet scaling.} Generation improves with the
number of books at fixed alphabet (64 to 128 books moved the end-to-end
critic from 0.806 to 0.834, with the gain preserved through decoration
and sweep) and degrades with alphabet depth at matched bits
($64\times32$ at 320 bits reaches 0.7969 against 0.8344 for
$128\times16$ at 512 bits). A global code removes tile seams by
construction: the mean pixel step across tile boundaries, relative to
elsewhere, is 0.91--1.07 for the global code against 1.768 for a
per-patch code measured on a predecessor of this pipeline, with real
images at 1.100 (Fig.~\ref{fig:backbone}).

\emph{(b) Codec rate--distortion.} At rate parity (512 bits) the
global dropout-AQ code reconstructs better than a per-patch codec
(relMSE 0.0500 against 0.0576 for a per-patch codec measured on that
same predecessor), so positional locality does not help
compression here, and the two-level residual construction reaches
0.0382 (Fig.~\ref{fig:codec}). Plain additive quantization ($p = 1$)
degrades with scale through dead atoms and stalled search, while
keep-$p$ is a clean dial: $p = 0.75$ is the compression frontier, and
$p = 0.5$ concedes roughly 20\% on the floor for three to five times
the cross-address coherence that generation feeds on.

Three differences carry the comparison. \emph{Mixing versus one pass
and one sweep:} the DTM chain exists to keep every step easy to mix,
and the residual difficulty moves into how many steps and iterations
the chain needs. CHILL ZEN's procedure contains no mixing phase, so we
report no mixing time, and its open problem is the draw. \emph{The
randomness source:} the DTCA obtains randomness from a dedicated
circuit whose correlation time is fixed at design; CHILL ZEN obtains
it from the read itself, at a temperature chosen per instruction,
including zero. What the state-dependence of the device noise
contributes is measured: replacing it with a flat noise at matched
median leaves the critic unchanged within seed spread (0.8417 against
that run's control at 0.8350) and costs diversity (0.4384 against 0.4671), so the
calibration supplies dispersion rather than label agreement.
\emph{Rendering:} the DTCA emits spins, and reaches grayscale through
a multi-spin embedding and color through a separately trained neural
decoder; a CHILL ZEN sample decodes through the fixed linear map of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:decode}). Sequential steps alone do not compare the two
systems, since the work per step differs and neither is fabricated at
target scale, which is why the accounting continues in joules.

\subsection{Energy}\label{sec:energy}

% 11_operating_point.py, 12_energy_model.py, 13_energy_frontier.py,
% 22_binary_energy.py, 28_physical_energy.py; Fig. 9: 15_energy_figure.py.
For a read whose noise is thermally limited, the referred noise and
the dissipation are
\begin{equation}
\sigma^2 = \frac{2 \kB T}{t V^2 G_\Sigma}, \qquad
E = V^2 G_\Sigma t,
\end{equation}
where $G_\Sigma$ is the selected-pool conductance and $t$ the read
pulse width, and their product eliminates the device entirely:
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{read}} \, \sigma^2 = 2 \kB T .
\label{eq:ethermal}
\end{equation}
The energy of one stochastic sample at noise level $\sigma$ is set by
thermodynamics alone, for a pooled lane read exactly as for a single
device. The identity governs the lane's share of a hot read's noise,
of order $10^3$--$10^4\,\kB T$ per read at the lane noise of the
deployed system; the DTCA obtains each random bit from a dedicated
circuit dissipating about 2\,fJ, of order $10^5\,\kB T$
\cite{jelincic2025dtm}. At the operating point of
Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} the lane's share is a minority of the draw's
variance, so the identity fixes the cost of the cheap part; the
comparator's share is counted through its own per-read energy below.

The cold reads dominate the per-image energy, because a hot read is
cheap by Eq.~(\ref{eq:ethermal}) and a cold one must clear a sense
margin over the flicker floor. We model the deployed system's
per-image energy from its exact instruction census (2048 hot
autoregressive reads, 1568 hot patch reads, 5664 cold sweep reads, and
784 cold reads for the decode: 10\,064 lane reads over four banks
totalling about $2.8\times10^{7}$ synapse pairs, or
$5.6\times10^{7}$ memristors), the measured pool conductances, a
10\,mV sense floor, settling-limited read timing ($t \geq 5RC$ at
100\,fF of lane capacitance), and periphery terms (line charging, one
comparator per lane at 1\,fJ, address selection and clocking)
mirroring the decomposition of Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm}. The
memristor's write compliance current sets where in the conductance
range the lane operates, and the fabricated device families span two
bands: 50\,k$\Omega$--1\,M$\Omega$ (tungsten) and 1--30\,M$\Omega$
(tin and chromium at write compliance at or below 1\,$\mu$A)
\cite{knowm2026datasheet}. The decode is in the census because the DTM
has none for binarized Fashion-MNIST, its spins being the pixels; it is
the pooled read of Eq.~(\ref{eq:pool}) over 784 output lanes, about
8\% of the reads.

The model is a physical model, not a measurement, at the same
order-of-magnitude fidelity as the comparison points: theirs
extrapolates from one fabricated RNG test circuit and foundry process
models; ours from the measured parameters of commercially sold
memristors and estimates for lane periphery. We therefore state it at
three operating points and report all three, including the one at
which the deployed system costs more per image than the DTM chain
(Table~\ref{tab:energy}).

inspect. With that stated: 18.73 is better than every
probabilistic-computer point they report (DTM best 24.9, MEBM best
33.5) and better than their GAN series (best 26.3). It is not better
than their two strongest GPU baselines, a VAE at 17.9 and a DDPM whose
best decoded point is 12.2 (their axis clips it; the best visible point
is 16.4), which sit seven to eleven orders of magnitude to the right in
energy. The binary-trained 11.10 sits 6.8 below the VAE and 1.1 below
the DDPM's best decoded point, which we read as parity with the DDPM
rather than a win.

% Audits: head_to_head/30_bernoulli_baseline.py, 31_free_draw.py,
% 32_audit_score.py, 33_binary_memorization.py.
\emph{Three audits.} First, every CHILL ZEN row is drawn with the label
given, 512 per class. The replication scores its own runs both ways,
and its clamped best beats its free best in all 44 of its shipped runs
by a median of 3.0 FID; whether their Fig.~1 reports the free or the
clamped mode is not stated. We measured our own free mode, sampling the
label from the uniform class prior and then running the same draw, and
the numbers do not move (binary-trained 11.05 against 11.10, grayscale
18.32 against 18.73), because the label prior enters by construction
and nothing has to learn the class balance. Second, the metric is not
soft for trivial models of this data: a per-class independent-Bernoulli
sampler scores FID 180.5 through the identical path, and 258.1 without
class information. Third, the binary-trained renders are not memorized:
all 5120 are distinct, one is a copy of a training image where held-out
real images copy training images at 13 per 10\,000, and their
nearest-training-neighbour Hamming distribution matches that of
held-out real images (median 24 bits against 23).

\emph{The convention costs more than the model.} The binarization
threshold is a property of the data, not of any generator, and changing
it moves FID further than the difference between the systems compared.
Scored against their reference, the same 60\,000 real train images
binarized at 0.5 rather than 0.1 give FID 29.75, worse than their
8-step DTM. Figure~\ref{fig:binarization} shows why: at 0.5 the smooth
mid-tones of a render straddle the threshold and break into speckle,
while at 0.1 they resolve into clean silhouettes. A number scored under
an unstated data convention is not comparable to anything, ours
included, and the validation above is why we are willing to state this
comparison at all.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item All results are emulator results with bit-exact lane semantics.
  The memristors and crossbars exist as fabricated hardware; the
  lane-scale system does not. Energy numbers are modeled, not
  measured, and are reported as a range for that reason; at the most
  conservative operating point the system costs 2.2 times more per
  image than the DTM chain. The model covers read and periphery energy
  per image and not die area, select-transistor overhead, leakage, or
  the one-time energy of programming the roughly $5.6\times10^{7}$
  memristors the four banks require.
\item The comparator is modeled, not designed. Its noise is one flat
  term set per read, its offset is assumed absorbed by learning, and
  its energy is one estimated constant. The operating points of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} state what a comparator must supply; they
  do not show a circuit that supplies it. The flicker term's
  extrapolation to nanosecond pulses is likewise unverified.
\item Cold reads are treated as $T = 0$. Physically they sit at the
  flicker floor with the comparator at full bias; we treat the two as
  the same because the winning lane of a WTA Group clears its rivals
  by about 46 times that floor. The energy model does not make the
  simplification and counts every cold read's sense margin.
\item Decode, render, and judging run in host floating point. The
  decode is counted as a lane operation in the energy census, with the
  magnitudes of its pooled read proxied by the joint sweep's; moving
  the decoder atoms into lanes is ongoing work.
\item The quality bar of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} is a pixel-critic
  agreement score, not FID, because Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm}
  establishes no grayscale reference. The end-to-end critic exceeds
  both the reconstruction arm and the real bar; we read that as label
  agreement, have not identified what in the draw's distribution the
  critic responds to beyond what Sec.~\ref{sec:gap} measures, and note
  that the contrast alarm is not closed (std-ratio 1.15 against 1.10).
\item Our systems are fit on a 68\,000-image split of the full
  70\,000-image dataset, while the reference statistics of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} are over the 60\,000-image train split
  that Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm} trains on; the training sets are
  neither identical nor the same size. The generated side of every FID
  is 5120 images and carries the small-sample floor of 2.14 that the
  reference side does not; the DTM numbers are scored at the same $n$.
\item The one-level frontier was scored at the operating point found
  for the two-level stack and was not swept itself, so
  Table~\ref{tab:headtohead} understates what it would reach on its
  own sweep. We report no gradient-trained control on this code and
  connectivity, so the contribution of the local lane rule is not
  separated from that of the code and the recipe; the paper does not
  claim the local rule outperforms gradient training. Conditioning is
  label-clamped, and the unconditional mode of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} supplies the class prior rather than
  learning it.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Two machines aimed at the same problem disagree about the thermostat.
The DTM keeps every step of a chain easy to mix, and the chain is the
cost. CHILL ZEN stores the posterior in the device, reads the code out
hot one address at a time, and reconciles the result in one cold
sweep. Scored by the DTM's own evaluation code against its own
reference statistics, the two-level system reaches FID 18.7 against
24.9, and 11.1 when trained on the binarized data, at a modeled energy
that runs from 838 times less to 2.2 times more depending on which
memristor band the reader grants.

The finding we did not expect is about where the temperature comes
from. The substrate's randomness is real and calibrated, but a lane
read pools thousands of weights, and the same pooled conductance that
sharpens the answer damps the noise. At the sense floor the devices
deliver about a third of what a generative draw needs, and no read
voltage or pulse width recovers the rest. The comparator that resolves
the read does. Its input-referred noise, referred to the read voltage,
is the draw's temperature: it sets the operating point, it is
indifferent to the read voltage at a fixed ratio, and its flat shape
feeds the start of the chain, which is where the draw starves. A
comparator for this architecture therefore has a requirement a
comparator for a memory does not: noise that can be set per
instruction, from near zero for a verdict to a few tenths of the read
voltage for a draw. That is a circuit-design constraint, and it is
stated here so it can be met or argued with.

What remains open is also stated. The residual error is in one bank,
the label$\to$code draw, and its signature is distributional. The lane-scale
hardware, and the comparator it needs, have not been fabricated. None
of the results used a gradient, a mixing phase, or a random-number
generator: one hot pass over the code, then a sweep at zero, and the
reconciliation is the computation.
