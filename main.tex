\title{From one to zero: Comparator-Heated In-memory Lane Learning
  with Zero-Ended Noise}

\author{M. Alexander Nugent}
\affiliation{Knowm Inc.}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We present CHILL ZEN---Comparator-Heated In-memory Lane Learning
with Zero-Ended Noise---a generative architecture for probabilistic
in-memory hardware. Each weight is a differential memristor pair, and
a read returns the weight plus noise. The sampling temperature is an
argument of the read instruction, over a range that reaches zero, so
generation is one hot autoregressive pass over a discrete address code
followed by one zero-temperature reconciliation sweep, with no mixing
phase. A lane read pools hundreds of pairs, and pooling quenches the
read's own noise down to the kT/C floor of the output node: at the
10\,mV read the devices deliver under two-fifths of the noise the draw
needs on the first read and under a tenth thereafter, so the draw's
randomness has to come from the periphery. It comes from the
comparator that resolves each read, one per lane, with settable
input-referred noise; at the operating point that circuit supplies
most of the draw's variance. Sweeping its noise
locates the operating point
and fixes a design requirement on the comparator. The energy case
against a mixing chain is a count: an image needs about 3600 noisy
reads here where an eight-step chain needs of order $10^{7}$
conditional updates. On grayscale Fashion-MNIST a two-level stack matches the
real bar on the critic on its reconstruction arm and scores above it
end-to-end, in about 131 sequential read steps per image. Binarized and scored under the evaluation protocol of the
denoising thermodynamic model of Jelin\v{c}i\v{c} \emph{et al.}, the
same system scores FID 18.1 at its FID-best comparator level against
24.9 for their deepest chain, at a
modeled $2\times10^{-9}$\,J per image against their
$1.57\times10^{-8}$\,J; trained directly on the binarized data, the
architecture scores FID 9.7.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The energy consumption of generative AI has become a first-order
constraint on its deployment, and it has prompted a search for
computers in which physics performs the sampling
\cite{conte2019thermodynamic}. Probabilistic bits
made from stochastic nanomagnets and related devices have run
optimization and learning workloads at very low energy
\cite{sutton2017intrinsic,borders2019integer,niazi2024training}, and
Jelin\v{c}i\v{c} \emph{et al.}\ \cite{jelincic2025dtm} recently
proposed a complete generative system---model class, architecture,
and energy analysis---on all-transistor probabilistic circuits.

That work states the central obstacle cleanly: the
mixing--expressivity tradeoff. As a monolithic energy-based model
(EBM) fits multimodal data better, energy barriers grow between the
modes, and the mixing time of any locally informed sampler grows with
them. Their answer is the Denoising Thermodynamic Model (DTM), a chain
of $T = 2$--$8$ EBMs that reverses a noising process in the manner of
diffusion models \cite{sohldickstein2015deep,ho2020ddpm} so that each
step stays easy to mix, and the Denoising Thermodynamic Computer
Architecture (DTCA), which implements each step on sparse bipartite
Boltzmann machines whose nodes are subthreshold-transistor
random-number generators (RNGs) with a measured decorrelation time
$\tau_0 \approx 100$\,ns \cite{jelincic2025dtm,freitas2025taming}.

Our answer to the same constraint is to make the sampling temperature
an argument of the read instruction, over a
range that reaches zero, so that generation is one hot pass over the
code followed by one cold sweep rather than a mixing chain. We call the system CHILL
ZEN, for Comparator-Heated In-memory Lane Learning with Zero-Ended
Noise.

The randomness is the harder half of the design. A stored weight pair
carries its own read noise, and pooling many pairs on one lane
quenches that noise in the same proportion that it sharpens the
answer, down to the kT/C floor of the lane's output node: at the
10\,mV read the deployed lanes deliver under two-fifths of the noise
a generative draw needs on the first read and under a tenth
thereafter, and no read voltage a comparator can resolve recovers the
rest. The draw's temperature therefore has to be supplied by the
periphery. It is supplied by the comparator that resolves each read,
one per lane, with settable input-referred noise; at the operating
point the comparator carries most of the draw's variance, over nine tenths after the first read, and the pooled
devices carry the remainder. In this respect CHILL ZEN and the DTCA are alike: each
obtains its randomness from a noise circuit at every node. What
separates them is count. One CHILL ZEN image needs about 3600 noisy
reads, where an eight-step DTM chain needs of order $10^{7}$
conditional updates, every one of them a random draw, and that count,
rather than the cost of a random bit, is where the energy accounting
of Sec.~\ref{sec:energy} comes from. We state the comparator's design
requirement and measure the operating point.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item CHILL ZEN, a generative model class for lane hardware, defined
  by its code, its paired generator and repair banks, and its
  one-pass, one-sweep procedure (Sec.~\ref{sec:model});
\item the two-level result on grayscale Fashion-MNIST
  \cite{xiao2017fashion}, with the residual error localized to one
  component, the label$\to$code draw (Secs.~\ref{sec:headline} and
  \ref{sec:gap});
\item the comparator-noise result: pooled reads quench their own
  noise, the comparator supplies the draw temperature, and a sweep of
  its noise setting fixes the operating point and the design
  requirement (Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator});
\item a comparison with the DTM/DTCA as design points, a modeled
  energy accounting, and a head-to-head on binarized Fashion-MNIST
  scored with the released evaluation code of
  Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm} (Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison}).
\end{enumerate}
Every result is emulated lane computation: device models with the
read-noise physics of Sec.~\ref{sec:substrate}, every read and adapt
issued as a lane instruction, and decode, render, and judging in
host-side floating point.

\section{The substrate}\label{sec:substrate}

\subsection{The kT-bit and its read noise}\label{sec:ktbit}

The storage and sampling element is a differential pair of metastable
memristors read through a voltage divider. With conductances $G_a$
and $G_b$ and read voltage $V$, the divider reports
\begin{equation}
V_y = V\,\frac{G_a - G_b}{G_a + G_b}, \qquad
y = \frac{V_y}{V} \in [-1, 1],
\label{eq:divider}
\end{equation}
and the pair carries two state variables,
\begin{equation}
w = \frac{G_a - G_b}{G_a + G_b}, \qquad m = G_a + G_b .
\label{eq:wm}
\end{equation}
The weight $w$ is the balance of the pair and the magnitude $m$ is its
total. The magnitude is the weight's inertia: a feedback pulse moves
$w$ in proportion to $1/m$, so the effective learning rate falls on
the Robbins--Monro $1/n$ schedule with no schedule stored anywhere.
It is also a count of evidence. Forward feedback increments one
conductance by a quantum, which is the conjugate update of a Beta
distribution, so the pair holds a Beta posterior with mean and
concentration
\begin{equation}
\mu = \frac{G_a}{G_a + G_b}, \qquad \kappa = G_a + G_b, \qquad
\sigma^2 = \frac{\mu(1-\mu)}{\kappa + 1}.
\label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}

A read returns the stored weight plus noise. The noise has a thermal
term and a flicker term from the devices, and a third term from the
comparator that resolves the read, summed in quadrature:
\begin{align}
\sigma_{\mathrm{thermal}} &\propto \frac{\sqrt{\kB T}}{V\sqrt{m}},
\qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{flicker}} \propto \frac{1 - w^2}{\sqrt{m}},
\nonumber \\
\sigma_{\mathrm{comparator}} &= \frac{v_n}{V},
\nonumber \\
\sigma_y &= \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{thermal}}^2 +
                  \sigma_{\mathrm{flicker}}^2 +
                  \sigma_{\mathrm{comparator}}^2}.
\label{eq:noise}
\end{align}
The thermal term rides as $1/V$, so the read voltage is a
per-instruction temperature dial, and at full read voltage the device
terms sit at the flicker floor (Fig.~\ref{fig:noise}). We call such a
read cold and treat it as $T = 0$. That is a simplification, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} measures what it costs: on the states a cold
sweep actually reads, the winning lane of a winner-take-all group
clears its rivals by about ten times the physical floor at the median,
about 7\% of the groups sit within one floor of a tie, and running the
sweeps at that floor flips those near-ties and moves no judge. At a
fixed pulse width both device
terms fall as $1/\sqrt{m}$, and the flicker term carries $(1 - w^2)$,
so a read's scatter narrows the two ways the posterior of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:beta}) narrows: with accumulated evidence, and toward the
certain ends. The forms match and the exponents do not; a hot read is
a sample whose width tracks the weight's own uncertainty rather than a
draw from the posterior. At the settling-limited pulse the reads of
this paper use, $t = 5C/G_\Sigma$, the thermal term takes a different
form: the pooled conductance cancels, and what remains is the kT/C
noise of the lane's output node,
$\sigma^2_{\mathrm{thermal}} \approx \kB T / (C_{\mathrm{node}} V^2)$,
which no longer depends on the devices at all. Pooling still quenches
it, through the wire rather than the conductance, because the node
grows with the number of pairs pooled on it (Appendix~\ref{app:energy},
$C_{\mathrm{node}} \propto K$). The sign of one subthreshold read is a
writable coin, a bit with $P(+) = \Phi(w/\sigma_y)$ whose bias is the
stored weight.

The comparator term is different in kind. It carries no dependence on
$w$ or $m$, only on the comparator's input-referred noise $v_n$
referred to the read voltage. That noise is kT/C noise on the
regeneration nodes plus the preamplifier's own thermal noise
\cite{razavi2015strongarm}. It is a settable circuit parameter: it
rises when the preamplifier bias is
starved or a deliberate noise source is added at the input, and in a
clocked comparator when the latch is strobed earlier. It falls only at a cost in area and power,
down to a floor set by the comparator's design class. We model it as an
instruction-time setting, a trim register the read carries like its
voltage and its pulse width, so each read instruction carries its draw
temperature. The comparator's
input offset is a separate quantity, a static per-lane bias set by
transistor matching \cite{pelgrom1989matching}; being static, it
belongs to a one-time per-lane trim rather than to learning, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} states the budget.

The comparator term matters because of pooling, which
Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} measures. A lane read pools the magnitudes
of every pair it selects, so a well-taught pool is quiet twice over:
the flicker term averages over more evidence, and the thermal term is
the kT/C noise of a node that grows with every pair pooled on it. What
is left is a floor set by the read voltage and the node capacitance.
At the 10\,mV read the deployed lanes deliver about 0.02--0.04 on
their own as modeled, and up to about 0.07 on the chain's first read
by the geometry of Appendix~\ref{app:energy}, where the node is a
dozen femtofarads; the draw needs 0.1 or more, and reaching it from
the node alone would take a read of about a millivolt, below what any
comparator's fixed errors allow. The comparator supplies the
difference, and the temperature schedule of a CHILL ZEN generation is
a bias schedule on that one circuit per lane: hot reads starve the
preamplifier, cold reads run it at full bias.

Using device variability as the sampler rather than designing it out
is established. Dalgaty \emph{et al.}\ \cite{dalgaty2021insitu} run
Markov chain Monte Carlo in a 16{,}384-device array by treating the
cycle-to-cycle variability of a memristive write as the proposal
distribution, and Cai \emph{et al.}\ \cite{cai2020hopfield} use
intrinsic device noise as the annealing source in a memristor Hopfield
network. Both draw on a single device's variability. In
Eq.~(\ref{eq:noise}) the noise is a property of the instruction and of
one peripheral circuit as well as of the device: the same weight can
be read hot or cold, the range reaches zero, and once hundreds of
pairs are pooled on a lane the peripheral circuit is the larger term.

\subsection{The neural lane}\label{sec:lane}

A neural lane is an array of unit-crossbar pairs pooled on a shared
2-1 divider. By Kirchhoff's laws the selected pairs report
\begin{equation}
y = \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} (G_{a,i} - G_{b,i})}
         {\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} (G_{a,i} + G_{b,i})}
  = \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} m_i w_i}
         {\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}} m_i},
\label{eq:pool}
\end{equation}
the magnitude-weighted, which is to say inverse-variance-weighted,
average of the selected weights. Selection is an
Activation Address Tuple (AAT): an ordered tuple of integers, one per
address space, and the tuple is the wiring pattern pin for pin. One
consequence sets the model class: a space's influence on a read is
its share of the pool's total magnitude, roughly $1/K$ when $K$
spaces are active, so an address that carries no measured value takes
influence from the ones that do, and an address that must condition
the read has to be replicated across several spaces.

Instruction codes in the kT-RAM instruction set (Table~\ref{tab:isa})
are two letters: the first is the drive direction (F forward, R
reverse), the second the operation. Forward feedback accumulates
magnitude and reverse feedback depletes it; every working routine
pairs a forward operation with a reverse one.

\subsection{WTA Groups and the lane routines}\label{sec:routines}

Lanes are organized into winner-take-all (WTA) Groups, one lane per
symbol of an address space, and three routines cover everything in
this paper; each is described in depth in an online chapter series
\cite{knowm2026blog}. In the \emph{supervised three-case rule}, all
lanes of a group are read with \texttt{FF}; the labeled lane receives
\texttt{RH}; a wrong lane that fired receives \texttt{RL} (hard) or
nothing beyond the paired reverse read (soft); a correctly silent lane
receives \texttt{RF}. Cold argmax readout of a group taught this way
is a classifier at parity with logistic regression on the identical
encoding (Iris: 0.962 against 0.961 over twenty splits). Hard feedback
carves a margin; soft feedback keeps every answer the data supports,
so a soft bank read hot draws from the distribution the data left in
its weights, and the chain rule runs as sequential lane reads: clamp
what is known, read one space, commit, continue. \emph{RankCut
readout} reports the above-threshold lanes of a group in rank order
from a shared falling reference and one comparator per lane, with no
per-lane analog-to-digital converter. The \emph{WTA competitive rule}
drops the label and targets the read winner, with exclusion and
recruitment in the spirit of conscience mechanisms
\cite{desieno1988conscience}, so a group learns a codebook with no
teacher.

\subsection{The emulator}\label{sec:emulator}

All results are emulated lane computation on
\texttt{ktram-neural-core} \cite{knowm2026emulator}: device models
with the read-noise physics above, a torch runtime bit-exact against
the reference core, and every read and adapt issued as a lane
instruction. The emulator's crossbar fidelity is ideal: a read returns
the selected pair of each space alone, with no contribution from the
unselected devices of the unit crossbar
(Limitation~\ref{lim:sneak}). The physical memristors and crossbars
exist as fabricated and commercially sold hardware
\cite{knowm2026datasheet}; the lane-scale system does not yet. The emulator's device noise gain
(0.02 at its reference operating point) is pinned throughout and never
raised to drive a hot read. A hot read is driven by the read voltage
and by the comparator term of Eq.~(\ref{eq:noise}), both physical
operating points.

\section{The model class}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{The code}\label{sec:code}

Images are represented in a per-position dropout additive-quantization
(dropout-AQ) code: $k$ books of $S$ atoms at keep probability $p$, in
the lineage of vector and additive quantization
\cite{gray1984vq,jegou2011product,babenko2014additive} and of
generation over discrete codes
\cite{vandenoord2017vqvae,chang2022maskgit}. A code is a point in
$\A_S^k$ ($k$ address spaces of $S$ symbols each), mirroring the
codeword space $\mathbb{F}_q^n$ of coding theory. The training
objective marginalizes Bernoulli address deletion,
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}_z \Bigl\| x - b - \sum_j z_j a_j \Bigr\|^2
&= \Bigl\| x - b - p \sum_j a_j \Bigr\|^2 \nonumber \\
&\quad + p(1-p) \sum_j \| a_j \|^2,
\label{eq:dropoutaq}
\end{align}
with $z_j \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$, and decoding, including of
partial states, is the fixed linear map
\begin{equation}
\hat{x} = b + p \sum_{j \in \mathrm{present}} a_j .
\label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
The keep probability is a coherence-versus-fidelity dial: $p = 1$
recovers plain additive quantization \cite{babenko2014additive}, with
the best floor and brittle partial states, and $p < 1$ trades floor
for cross-address redundancy. Partial codes are in-distribution by
construction: a half-filled
address state decodes to a coherent soft image, which is what makes
hole-filling a learnable lane task.

Stripped of the hardware, the model class is generation over a linear
additive-quantization codebook with masked-context training and a
parallel reconciliation sweep, which places it beside MaskGIT
\cite{chang2022maskgit} and the VQ-VAE lineage
\cite{vandenoord2017vqvae}. The decode is a fixed
linear map rather than a learned network, which is what makes partial
states meaningful and the energy accounting of Sec.~\ref{sec:energy}
possible. And each level pairs a soft bank that draws with a hard
bank that reconciles, both taught by local lane instructions, where a
masked transformer uses one network and a confidence schedule.

\subsection{Paired banks and the generation procedure}\label{sec:banks}

Each level of a CHILL ZEN stack pairs a soft generator bank G, read
hot, with a hard repair bank R, taught on a corrupted pool and applied
cold in one full-commit parallel sweep. The two-level system evaluated here has a
\emph{backbone} (level 1: a global code over the whole canvas, with
banks G1 and R1) and a \emph{patch level} (level 2: a residual code on
a grid of patch slots, with bank G2 and a cross-level joint repair
bank). The \emph{render} is the backbone decode plus the patch decode.
Generation runs as follows (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item Clamp the label, replicated across six address spaces.
\item Backbone draw: an autoregressive hot read over the 128 global
  books at the operating point of Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator},
  $(V, v_n) = (10\,\mathrm{mV}, 2\,\mathrm{mV})$, the critic's
  optimum on the declared grid (Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} reports FID
  at its own best level of that grid), each read sampling
  $p(z_m \mid z_{<m}, \ell)$ through the read noise, followed by one
  cold R1 sweep.
\item Patch level: one parallel hot G2 read, at the same operating
  point, over all 49 residual patch slots under the held global code.
\item One cold cross-level joint sweep reconciles both levels: all
  226 addresses are targets, and the sweep may flip globals bottom-up
  and patches top-down in the same parallel read.
\item Decode and render: the fixed linear map of Eq.~(\ref{eq:decode}),
  run in host floating point in this paper's emulation and priced in
  Sec.~\ref{sec:energy} as the lane read it is designed to be.
\end{enumerate}

The sequential step count is 128 autoregressive reads plus three
parallel sweeps, 131 sequential read steps per image, and the decode of
step 5 is one more parallel read when it runs on lanes. The
temperature schedule is two-valued and per-instruction, stated in the
comparator's noise setting at a shared read voltage: $v_n = 2$\,mV for
draws and $v_n \to 0$ for verdicts, inside one image.
% 37_dose_chain.py (and --quench): the sweep chained to eight doses.
The joint sweep runs once by design. A second and further cold doses
lower the critic by about 0.01 per dose, from 0.90 to 0.81 by the
eighth, pull diversity and tone toward class prototypes, and do the
same to real encoded codes, so the chain's attractors are not the
data. Read noise in the sweep at a tenth of the read voltage holds the
critic at the one-dose level but not the diversity, and at the draw's
own operating point the parallel sweep washes out.

\section{Training and results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Setup and judge}\label{sec:setup}

The benchmark is grayscale Fashion-MNIST \cite{xiao2017fashion},
split 68\,000 train and 2\,000 evaluation. The judge
reports four numbers: \emph{critic}, the label-agreement rate of a
frozen pixel-space multilayer perceptron
($784\to256\to\mathrm{ReLU}\to10$) on the generated images, where the
real-reference row is the bar and parity with it is the target;
\emph{div}, within-class sample diversity (fraction of pixel pairs
differing by more than $1/16$); \emph{tone}, the spread of per-image
foreground mean level within class; and \emph{std-ratio}, foreground
contrast relative to real, with an alarm margin of 1.10. No FID appears
in this section; the FID comparison with Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm}
is made in Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead}, on binarized renders, under that
work's own protocol.

Teaching runs entirely by lane instructions and no gradients are
computed anywhere. Soft G banks teach on Bernoulli-masked codes with
the label always held; hard R banks teach on realistically corrupted
pools (marginal corruption plus G fills); the joint bank teaches
on two-level corrupted states. Each bank is probed per epoch and the
best-probe snapshot is kept. The fills are drawn through the same read
that generates: the emulator's device noise at the 10\,mV read, with
the comparator's trim register supplying the temperature. The fill
level is a knob of its own, and it was set by measurement rather than
by matching the operating point: fills drawn at code 45, the
operating point at the time, taught markedly worse banks than hotter
fills, and the
end-to-end critic rose with the fill level, 0.857 at code 45, 0.877 at
95, 0.878 at 145 and 0.890 at code 245, flattening toward the
register's ceiling, which is where teaching stands. The binary-trained
banks of Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} teach at the same level, carried
over from this sweep rather than swept on that stack. Two choices in that recipe, holding the label and committing a single
cold dose, follow the pool-share argument of Sec.~\ref{sec:lane}.

\subsection{The two-level result}\label{sec:headline}

The deployed system is a 904-bit code plus the clamped label: a
$128\times16$ global backbone at keep-$p$ 0.5 (512 bits) and a
$2\times16$ residual patch code on a $7\times7$ grid of
$4\times4$-pixel slots (392 bits). Table~\ref{tab:headline} gives the
verdict as mean $\pm$ seed spread (max $-$ min) over three fresh seeds
at $n = 1000$; the spread is the evaluation noise at this $n$. The
reconstruction arm replaces the draw with the code of a real image and
runs every other component as deployed. Its critic matches the real
bar within seed spread, so everything after the draw adds no error
measurable by this classifier; its contrast does not match, since the
std-ratio of 1.13 exceeds the 1.10 alarm on this arm as it does
end-to-end. The end-to-end arm, drawn from the label alone,

\subsection{The comparator sets the draw temperature}\label{sec:comparator}

Every hot read in this paper runs at a modeled read voltage of
$V = 10$\,mV, with the pulse width settling-limited,
$t_b = \max(2\,\mathrm{ns}, 5RC)$. Ten millivolts is where the
comparator's fixed errors, a trimmed offset of about 1\,mV and the
repeatable kickback of Appendix~\ref{app:energy}, are a tenth of the
signal (Sec.~\ref{sec:ktbit}); we call it the sense floor. At that
read the lanes alone deliver a device read noise of 0.02--0.04 across
the backbone chain as modeled, with flicker 5--70\% of the variance:
dominant on the chain's first read, where the pool is a single book,
and falling to 5\% by the last as thermal takes over. The thermal
term is nearly flat in chain position in the model because the
settling time and the pooled conductance move together, which is the
kT/C form of Sec.~\ref{sec:ktbit} at the model's fixed 100\,fF of node
capacitance. The geometry of Appendix~\ref{app:energy} puts the node
at about 12\,fF on the first read and 161--278\,fF thereafter, so by
that geometry the first read is noisier than modeled, about 0.07, and
the later reads are as modeled. Either way the devices cannot be made
to supply more: the node's kT/C caps what a read's own physics can
deliver, at under two-fifths of the noise the draw turns out to need
on the first read and under a tenth thereafter, and reaching the
draw's noise from the node alone would take a read of about a
millivolt. The comparator term $v_n / V$ of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:noise})---flat in $y$ and $m$ and settable by circuit
design (Table~\ref{tab:comparator})---can be raised.

\emph{The sweep.} We swept $v_n / V$ at $V = 10$\,mV from 0 (lanes
only) to 0.20 on the grayscale end-to-end draw and to 0.50 on the
binary-trained two-level draw of Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead}, holding
every other component as deployed. The lanes alone fall short in both
stacks (Fig.~\ref{fig:comparatorsweep}), each
improves as the comparator's noise is raised, and each has an interior
optimum, at $v_n / V = 0.20$ for grayscale and $0.2$--$0.3$ for
binary; the binary optimum sits on the two points added to the grid
after a first pass, and a 7.5\,mV point swept before the level was a
register lies above the register's ceiling and is not reported. The
figure also marks a control, the same draw with the
emulator's device noise gain raised by hand. That gain is a device
calibration, not an operating knob, and no circuit produces that
noise; the control is a reference only.

The swept level is not a free parameter. The emulator carries it as an
8-bit trim register, $v_n = 100\,\mu\mathrm{V} + \mathrm{code} \times
20\,\mu\mathrm{V}$ over codes 0--255, so each level is reported as a
register code, and the grid is codes 3, 20, 45, 95, 145 and 245, of
which the grayscale critic sweep runs the first four and the grayscale
FID grid of Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} the first five plus the
device-only point. The
register is a modeling convenience, with its step chosen so that
these levels fall on exact codes; it validates nothing about a part.
There is no zero setting---no comparator reaches zero
input-referred noise---so $v_n / V = 0$ is the device-only law, a
statement about the model rather than a register value. At $V = 10$\,mV
the register's ceiling of 5.20\,mV caps $v_n / V$ at 0.52, which is
where the sweep stops.

% Sweep: 23_comparator_sweep.py, 24_binary_comparator_sweep.py.
At the optima the comparator
supplies 97--99\% of the read's variance in the grayscale draw and
99--100\% in the binary one as modeled; at the first read's node
capacitance of Appendix~\ref{app:energy} the grayscale share on that
read is about 90\%. A twin of the $v_n / V = 0.10$ point read
at $V = 50$\,mV, which quiets the lanes' thermal term five-fold,
matches the 10\,mV point on every judge (grayscale critic 0.8837
against 0.8840; binary FID 10.73 against 10.99): the comparator's
share alone sets the temperature. And the flat term has
the right dependence on chain position. The device noise falls as
$1/\sqrt{m}$, so a draw
run on device noise alone is loudest on the first reads of the chain,
where the pool is small, and quietest on the last; the comparator term
puts the same noise at every chain position. The chain's earliest
reads are the ones that starve (Sec.~\ref{sec:gap}).

\emph{The design requirement.} No comparator class reaches these
operating points on its own. A raw dynamic latch at a 10\,mV read
reaches $v_n / V \approx 0.2$ from its own kT/C and preamplifier
noise, so the grayscale optimum at 0.20 sits at the top of its range,
with the preamplifier starved, and the binary optimum at 0.2--0.3 sits
at or above it and needs either that latch starved further or a
dedicated noise source at the input. But the same latch carries
5--20\,mV of offset, which at a 10\,mV read is 0.5--2 of the full
range of a lane's output (Table~\ref{tab:comparator}), and no shift of
the weights cancels an offset larger than the range; with sixteen
lanes in a group each carrying its own, the winner would be the lane
with the lowest offset, not the lane with the highest weight. The
offset is static and the noise is not, so the two belong to different
mechanisms. CHILL ZEN therefore requires a comparator whose offset is
trimmed per lane, once, to below about a tenth of the read voltage
(1\,mV at 10\,mV) at each bias it runs at, and whose input-referred
noise is settable, from near zero for verdicts to
about 0.2--0.3 of the read voltage for draws, by a starved
preamplifier or an injected source. The offset budget can be relaxed
by reading hot at a higher voltage: the 50\,mV twin above shows the
operating point is set by the ratio $v_n / V$ and not by $V$, so a
50\,mV hot read loosens the offset requirement fivefold, at the cost
of needing 10\,mV rms of comparator noise, above the 5.20\,mV ceiling
of the register as modeled.

% Cold-read control: 35_cold_noise_control.py.
\emph{The cold reads.} The deployed generation runs its two cold
sweeps as noiseless reads, and physically a cold read is not
noiseless: at 50\,mV it carries the devices' flicker floor, the
settling-limited thermal term, and the comparator at its quietest
setting, together about 0.003 of the read. Measured on the states the
sweeps actually read, the median winner margin is 0.027 in the R1
sweep and 0.032 in the joint sweep, about ten floors, and 7\% of the
R1 groups and 6\% of the joint groups lie within one floor of a tie.
We therefore ran both sweeps through the physical read, with the
comparator at register codes 0, 10 and 45 (100\,$\mu$V, 300\,$\mu$V
and 1\,mV, or 0.002, 0.006 and 0.02 of the read), on the same hot
draws as the noiseless sweeps, three seeds at $n = 1000$. At the floor
the noise changes the verdict of 9 of the 128 R1 addresses and 12 of
the 226 joint addresses per image, and the end-to-end judge does not
move: critic 0.890 against 0.890 for the noiseless sweeps, diversity
0.550 against 0.549, tone and contrast unchanged. At 1\,mV, ten times
the floor, a quarter of the R1 verdicts and 52 of the 226 joint
verdicts change and the critic is still $0.893 \pm 0.024$ against
$0.890 \pm 0.012$. The addresses a cold read can flip are the near-ties,
and on this code a near-tie decodes the same either way. Treating the
cold read as $T = 0$ is therefore a simplification the judge cannot
see, with a margin of ten in the comparator's cold setting; the
head-to-head of Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} was not re-scored under it.
The margin has a price the other way: Appendix~\ref{app:energy} prices a
comparator quiet enough for code 45 at what the model charges and one
quiet enough for code 0 at ten times more.

The model has three caveats. The flicker term's extrapolation
from the emulator's 1\,$\mu$s reference to nanosecond pulses assumes
the $1/f$ band extends that far. The emulator's device constants are
placeholders pending measurement; the thermal term needs none, since
at the settling-limited pulse it is kT/C physics, and the flicker
term is the only device-specific quantity, 5--70\% of a lane share
that is itself a few percent of the draw. The model integrates the
thermal noise over the pulse, where a strobed comparator samples it,
which raises the lane's thermal term by a factor of about 1.6 and
does not change the conclusions above. And when the comparator
supplies most of a read's noise, the thermodynamic identity of
Sec.~\ref{sec:energy} describes only the lane's share; the
comparator's energy is counted separately there.

\subsection{The remaining error}\label{sec:gap}

The generation stack factors as draw $\to$ repair $\to$ decoration
$\to$ joint sweep $\to$ render, and the reconstruction arm of
Table~\ref{tab:headline} replaces exactly one component, the draw,
with ground truth. Its match to the real bar on the critic localizes
the residual error, conditional on the codec, to the label$\to$code
draw; its contrast does not match (std-ratio 1.13 against the 1.10
alarm), and Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} measures what the codec costs. The control
draw of Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} isolates the same bank from the
other direction: it scores $0.8273 \pm 0.0120$, and that entire gap
belongs to the draw. The judge is silent about the codec itself: the
reconstruction arm passes through it too, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} finds that the codec alone accounts for 12.3
FID points the critic scores as zero error. The two measurements bound
different components, and neither alone bounds the system.

% prefix reteach: 09_prefix_schedule.py.
The control draw's failure has a measured signature. Its per-book
symbol marginals deviate from the training marginals by 0.21 total
variation against a 0.05 real-sampling bar (0.33 at the worst book),
and in the earliest books the over-picked symbols are near-zero-contrast
atoms: the chain collapses its first draws onto flat modal atoms and
decodes them
too contrasty (foreground contrast 1.25 times real, against 1.13 on
the reconstruction arm). Re-encode self-consistency of the drawn codes
exceeds that of real codes, so the failure is distributional, and
reteaching the draw bank on its exact
run-time context schedule moved the critic by $-0.001$, at the noise
floor. Two noise schedules at the emulator's own scale test the
diagnosis. A linear schedule falling across the chain moves tone,
contrast, and diversity onto the reconstruction arm's values at a small
critic cost (0.8200), and a flat noise at the control's median is a
wash on the critic (0.8393 against 0.8273, inside the seed spread) and
costs diversity. Neither closes the
gap, because both stay at the lanes' own scale. The operating point of
Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} supplies a flat read seven to nine times
hotter (0.20 against the control's median of 0.023--0.030), and that is
what moves contrast onto the reconstruction arm's
value (std-ratio 1.14 against 1.13), narrows tone past it (1.59
against 2.00), and lifts the critic above it
(Table~\ref{tab:headline}). The contrast alarm is not fully closed:
end-to-end std-ratio is 1.14 against a 1.10 margin.

\subsection{Design studies}\label{sec:studies}

Two scaling studies set the deployed code, both measured on the
pipeline this paper describes.

\emph{(a) Capacity and alphabet scaling.} Generation improves with the
number of books at fixed alphabet (64 to 128 books moved the
backbone-only critic from 0.813 to 0.834 at keep-$p$ 0.5) and degrades
with alphabet depth at matched bits ($64\times32$ at 320 bits reaches
0.7875 against 0.8344 for $128\times16$ at 512 bits). A global code
removes tile seams by construction: the mean pixel step across tile
boundaries, relative to elsewhere, is 0.93--1.07 for the global code
against 1.768 for a
per-patch code measured on a predecessor of this pipeline, with real
images at 1.100 (Fig.~\ref{fig:backbone}).

\emph{(b) Codec rate--distortion.} At rate parity (512 bits) the
global dropout-AQ code at its compression frontier, keep-$p$ 0.75,
reconstructs better than a per-patch codec (relMSE 0.0500 against
0.0576 for a per-patch codec measured on that same predecessor), so
positional locality does not help compression here; the deployed
keep-$p$ 0.5 code gives that floor back (0.0612) for the coherence
below, and the two-level residual construction reaches 0.0382
(Fig.~\ref{fig:codec}). Plain additive quantization ($p = 1$)
degrades with scale through dead atoms and stalled search, while
keep-$p$ is a clean dial: $p = 0.75$ is the compression frontier, and
$p = 0.5$ concedes 12--22\% on the floor for 2.2--3.6 times the
cross-address coherence that generation relies on, the larger figures
at 128 books.

The DTM chain exists to keep every step easy to mix, and the residual

\subsection{Energy}\label{sec:energy}

% 12_energy_model.py, 13_energy_frontier.py, 22_binary_energy.py;
% Fig. 9: 15_energy_figure.py. The model is chill_zen/energy_geom.py;
% Appendix B gives every number behind this section.
For a read whose noise is thermally limited, the referred noise and
the dissipation are
\begin{equation}
\sigma^2 = \frac{2 \kB T}{t V^2 G_\Sigma}, \qquad
E = V^2 G_\Sigma t,
\end{equation}
where $G_\Sigma$ is the selected-pool conductance and $t$ the read
pulse width, and their product eliminates the device entirely:
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{read}} \, \sigma^2 = 2 \kB T .
\label{eq:ethermal}
\end{equation}
The energy of one stochastic sample at noise level $\sigma$ is set by
thermodynamics alone, for a pooled lane read exactly as for a single
device. For a comparator that samples the node at settling-limited
timing the realized product is $5 \kB T$ (Appendix~\ref{app:energy}).
Either way the identity governs only the
lane's share of a hot read's noise, of order $10^3$--$10^4\,\kB T$ per
read at the lane noise of the deployed system, and at the operating
point of Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} that share is 1--3\% of the draw's
variance. The comparator that supplies the rest costs about 10\,fJ per
lane read, of order $10^6\,\kB T$, which is more per random draw than
the DTCA pays: its RNG is measured at about 350\,aJ per bit, and its
whole cell update, RNG, bias, clock and communication together, is
about 2\,fJ \cite{jelincic2025dtm}. The identity therefore fixes the
cost of the cheap part and does not enter the accounting below, and
the comparison in joules is not won per random bit. It is won on
count: the accounting below charges about 3600 noisy reads per image,
where the chain charges $T K L^2 \approx 10^{7}$ conditional updates.

The per-image energy is a physical estimate from the stated geometry
of Appendix~\ref{app:energy}: the neural lane of
Ref.~\cite{knowm2026blog}, one $4 \times 4$ unit-crossbar pair per
address space on a 50\,nm pitch, the address of a read broadcast on
select lines across every lane of a bank, and the unselected lines
floating (Limitation~\ref{lim:sneak}). It charges three terms per
image: the delivery of each asserted address at the select supply,
$E_{\mathrm{sel}}$; the divider reads at $5\,C_{\mathrm{node}} V^2$
each, independent of the device's conductance, $E_{\mathrm{read}}$;
and one comparator and one lane buffer per lane read,
$E_{\mathrm{readout}}$, priced as a continuous-time comparator with
the alternatives in Table~\ref{tab:readout}; the rails are bounded
below $10^{-11}$\,J. It is applied to the exact operation count of the
deployed system, 10\,064 lane reads and $1.78\times10^{6}$ address
assertions over five banks totalling about $2.9\times10^{7}$ synapse
pairs, with constants that are each a stated dimension or a textbook
value (Table~\ref{tab:app-constants}). The decode is in the count. It
is the fixed linear map of Eq.~(\ref{eq:decode}), and a lane read
computes a linear map: with the codebook atoms stored as differential
pairs, one output lane per pixel, and every code address present at
the end of a generation, the pooled read of Eq.~(\ref{eq:pool})
returns the sum of the selected atoms at a fixed scale. It is about
8\% of the reads. This paper's emulation runs that map in host
floating point, as it does the render and the judging; the model
charges it as the 784 cold lane reads it is designed to be. Running it
on lanes, with the atoms quantized to a pair's resolution and read at
50\,mV, has not been emulated and its precision is not measured. The
DTM has no decode for binarized Fashion-MNIST because its spins are
the pixels. That is a property of a binary-pixel model rather than a
saving that carries: the DTM reaches grayscale through a multi-spin
embedding and color through a separately trained neural decoder
(Table~\ref{tab:designpoint}), neither priced in its energy figure,
and the system of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} was built grayscale and
binarized for the head-to-head. The model's address-delivery term is
what Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm} calls $E_{\mathrm{comm}}$ and
reports as about half of its budget.

8\%, and the divider reads 1\%. We therefore state the model at its

\subsection{A head-to-head on binarized Fashion-MNIST}\label{sec:headtohead}

Reference~\cite{jelincic2025dtm} reports quality as FID
\cite{heusel2017fid} on binarized Fashion-MNIST. Comparing against it
means binarizing our renders and scoring them the same way, which
requires knowing what the same way is. Their paper states the metric

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item All results are emulator results with bit-exact lane semantics.
    Energy numbers are modeled from a stated
  geometry, not measured, and are reported with their sensitivities for
  that reason. The dominant term, delivery of each read's address to
  the arrays at logic voltage, scales with the periphery's transistor
  pitch and the select supply, and at the model's pessimistic corner
  the advantage over the DTM chain falls to a factor of two. The model
  covers address delivery, read, and readout energy per image and not
  die area, leakage beyond the bound of Appendix~\ref{app:energy}, or
  the one-time energy of programming the roughly $5.7\times10^{7}$
  memristors the banks require.
\item\label{lim:sneak} The unit crossbar has no selector at the
  memristor and floats its unselected lines, so a symbol's read is
  44\% that symbol and 48\% the six symbols that share its row or
  column of the array (Appendix~\ref{app:energy},
  Sec.~\ref{app:sneak}), and a feedback pulse divides the same way.
  The emulator's crossbar fidelity is ideal, every read returns the
  selected pair alone, and every result of this paper is at that
  fidelity. Teaching closes its loop on the same read that inference
  uses, so the bias part of this crosstalk is the static non-ideality
  that in-the-loop learning absorbs; the loss of address resolution
  and the write disturb are not, and neither has been measured.
  Table~\ref{tab:sneak} prices the ways out; the $16 \times 1$
  selector column, which has no sneak path, is the geometry we would
  build and not the one this paper prices.
\item The comparator is a model; no circuit has been designed. Its noise is one flat
  term set per read, its offset is assumed trimmed per lane to below a
  tenth of the read voltage at each bias setting, and
  its energy is one estimated constant, with the readout circuit's
  alternatives priced in Table~\ref{tab:readout}. The operating points of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} state what a comparator must supply; they
  do not show a circuit that supplies it. The flicker term's
  extrapolation to nanosecond pulses is likewise unverified.
\item The quality bar of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} is a pixel-critic
  agreement score, not FID, because Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm}
  establishes no grayscale reference. The end-to-end critic exceeds
  both the reconstruction arm and the real bar, by a margin inside its
  own seed spread; we read that as label
  agreement, have not identified what in the draw's distribution the
  critic responds to beyond what Sec.~\ref{sec:gap} measures, and note
  that the contrast alarm is not closed (std-ratio 1.14 against 1.10).
\item Our systems are fit on a 68\,000-image split of the full
  70\,000-image dataset, while the reference statistics of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} are over the 60\,000-image train split
  that Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm} trains on; the training sets are
  neither identical nor the same size.
\item Every grayscale row of Table~\ref{tab:headtohead} is reported
  at its best level on a six-level comparator grid, selected by the
  same FID it reports; no finer grid and no held-out selection was
  run. We report no gradient-trained control on this code and
  connectivity, so the contribution of the local lane rule is not
  separated from that of the code and the recipe; the paper does not
  claim the local rule outperforms gradient training. Conditioning is
  label-clamped, and the unconditional mode of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:headtohead} supplies the class prior rather than
  learning it.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

CHILL ZEN generates by reading stored weights hot and reconciling them
cold, with the sampling temperature an argument of the read
instruction and zero in its range. The pooled devices cannot supply a
generative draw's temperature, because a pooled read's thermal noise
is the kT/C noise of its output node, so the comparator that resolves
the read supplies it, and its input-referred noise referred to the
read voltage is the draw's temperature. A comparator for this
architecture therefore has a requirement a comparator for a memory
does not: noise that can be set, from near zero for a verdict to a few
tenths of the read voltage for a draw, on top of an offset trimmed per
lane to below a tenth of the read voltage. The residual error is in
one bank, the label$\to$code draw, and its signature is
distributional. The level that maximizes the critic is not the level
that minimizes the FID for the same stack, and each arm is reported at
the level its own metric selected. The lane-scale hardware, and the
comparator it needs, have not been fabricated.

\section{The energy model}\label{app:energy}

The energy figures of Sec.~\ref{sec:energy} come from the model below,
a physical estimate from a stated geometry; the script that computes it
is named in Sec.~\ref{app:code}.

\subsection{Scope}\label{app:scope}

The model charges three terms per generated image: the delivery of
each read's address to the memristor arrays, the divider reads
themselves, and the per-lane readout. It excludes, and
Sec.~\ref{app:excluded} bounds, the rail
supplies, leakage, and comparator kickback. It excludes without a bound
the one-time programming of the weights and the die area, on the same
grounds as the comparison point of Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm}, which
excludes its own weight flash and counts per-sample energy only. The
basis of comparison is stated in Sec.~\ref{app:basis}.

\subsection{Geometry}\label{app:geometry}

The lane is the neural lane of Ref.~\cite{knowm2026blog}, Chapter~4. Its
element is the \emph{unit crossbar}: a small memristor crossbar with a
row multiplexer and a column multiplexer, which together couple exactly
one device between a source line and a drain line. There is no selector
transistor at the memristor; unselected rows and columns float. A synapse
is a pair of unit crossbars, the $a$-side between rail $V_a$ and the
output node and the $b$-side between the node and rail $V_b$. A lane is
$K$ such pairs, one per address space, with every pair's midpoint tied
to one output line $V_y$. Selection is the AAT: one address per space,
broadcast to every lane of a bank.

The array is an integrated crossbar with a 20\,nm via at each crossing
and the memristor in the via, on a 50\,nm pitch. Each space of the
deployed system has 16 symbols, so the nominal unit crossbar is the
smallest that holds them, a $4 \times 4$ array 0.20\,$\mu$m on a side
with two 4:1 pass-transistor multiplexers and $2 + 2$ address bits. A
$16 \times 16$ array is carried as a sensitivity.

Three consequences of that layout fix the capacitances
(Fig.~\ref{fig:app-geometry}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The select lines of a space run across the bank.} Every lane
  of a bank reads the same context, so the four row-select and four
  column-select lines of space $k$ cross all $L$ lanes. The $a$- and
  $b$-side multiplexers of a pair take the same address, so one row line
  and one column line serve both, each driving two pass gates per lane.
  Asserting a symbol charges two lines per (space, lane).
\item \emph{The multiplexers set the lane pitch, not the array.} Along a
  select line one lane is one unit crossbar wide: the 0.20\,$\mu$m array
  plus one stack of four pass transistors at the contacted transistor
  pitch $t$, so $p = 4d + 4t$ with $d$ the crossbar pitch. At $t =
  0.20\,\mu$m (a 28--40\,nm-class periphery) $p = 1.00\,\mu$m; at $t =
  0.06\,\mu$m (a 7\,nm-class periphery) $p = 0.44\,\mu$m. The via and
  crossbar pitch are therefore out of the argument: doubling $d$ to
  100\,nm moves the total by under 5\%.
\item \emph{The $V_y$ node is $2K$ lane pitches long and carries $8K$
  multiplexer drains.} The $a$ and $b$ arrays of a pair and the $K$
  spaces all stack along $V_y$. Its capacitance is
  $C_{\mathrm{node}} = K\,(2 p\, c_w + 8\, C_d)$ with $c_w$ the wire
  capacitance per length and $C_d$ the drain capacitance of an off pass
  transistor.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operation count}\label{app:census}

The operation count of the deployed two-level system is exact, taken
from the banks' lane and space counts. The generation of one image reads
five banks (Table~\ref{tab:app-census}). Two counts enter the model per
bank: the number of (space, lane) assertions, $L K$, which is what the
select lines must deliver, and the number of lane reads, which is what
the nodes and comparators must resolve. The backbone draw and patch draw
are hot reads at 10\,mV; the sweeps and the decode are cold reads at
50\,mV.

The backbone draw is 128 sequential steps, each reading a fresh group of
16 lanes with one more space asserted. Because the select lines run
bank-wide, asserting space $k$ for group $k$ also asserts it in the
groups that have already read; of the $g$ bank's 276\,576 assertions,
142\,336 do work. Segmenting the lines per group would save 48\% of
the $g$ bank's $E_{\mathrm{sel}}$, 7.5\% of the total
$E_{\mathrm{sel}}$, at the cost of a driver per group per space; the
model does not take it.

\subsection{Energy terms}\label{app:terms}

Per image,
\begin{equation}
E = E_{\mathrm{sel}} + E_{\mathrm{read}} + E_{\mathrm{readout}} +
    E_{\mathrm{rail}} .
\label{eq:app-total}
\end{equation}

\emph{Address delivery.} For each bank, each space asserted per image
charges one row line and one column line across every lane of the bank.
A line charged once and discharged once per image dissipates $C V^2$:
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{sel}} = \sum_{\mathrm{banks}} L\, K \cdot 2\,
  C_{\mathrm{seg}}\, V_{\mathrm{sel}}^2 ,
\qquad C_{\mathrm{seg}} = 2 C_g + p\, c_w .
\label{eq:app-sel}
\end{equation}
This is the term Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm} calls $E_{\mathrm{comm}}$,
the charging of the parasitic capacitance of the wires that carry a
cell's state to its neighbors, and reports as about half of its budget.

\emph{The divider read.} A lane read settles its $V_y$ node through the
selected devices and pass gates. With the pulse width set by settling,
$t = 5RC = 5\,C_{\mathrm{node}} / G_\Sigma$,
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{read}} = \sum_{\mathrm{reads}} V_{\mathrm{read}}^2\, G_\Sigma\, t
  = \sum_{\mathrm{reads}} 5\, C_{\mathrm{node}}\, V_{\mathrm{read}}^2 .
\label{eq:app-read}
\end{equation}
The pooled conductance cancels; the factor 5 is for rails at $\pm V$
about the node and a balanced pool, so that each side settles through
$V$. The read's energy is therefore
independent of the memristor's conductance band, of how a 20\,nm device
differs from a larger one, of the write compliance current, and of the
sneak conductance inside a floating-line unit crossbar. Inside a
$4 \times 4$ array with floating unselected lines the selected device
is $7/16$ of the conductance the node sees (Sec.~\ref{app:sneak}). For
the energy that is immaterial: sneak conductance shortens the settling
time and leaves Eq.~(\ref{eq:app-read}) unchanged. For the read it is
not; Sec.~\ref{app:sneak} derives what the node reads and prices the
ways out, and Limitation~\ref{lim:sneak} states what follows for the
results.

Equation~(\ref{eq:app-read}) also qualifies the thermodynamic identity
of Sec.~\ref{sec:energy}, Eq.~(\ref{eq:ethermal}). That identity, $E\,\sigma^2 = 2\kB T$, assumes the read
integrates the node over the full pulse. A comparator that samples the
node sees its $\kB T / C$ noise, $\sigma^2 = \kB T / (C_{\mathrm{node}}
V^2)$, and with $E = 5 C_{\mathrm{node}} V^2$ the realized product is
$5 \kB T$. The identity remains a floor on the lane's own contribution
to a hot read's noise; it does not enter the accounting.

\emph{Readout.} One comparator per lane resolves the read against the
group's shared falling reference (Sec.~\ref{sec:routines}). The model
prices it as a continuous-time comparator, a preamplifier and latch
biased at 200\,nA from the 0.8\,V supply for a 62.5\,ns read window,
10\,fJ per lane read, whose decision is free and whose bias is the
cost. The lane buffer of the readout circuit of
Ref.~\cite{knowm2026blog}, ``The RankCut recoder in hardware,'' a
follower biased at 100\,nA for the same window, costs 5\,fJ:
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{readout}} = N_{\mathrm{reads}} \times 15\,\mathrm{fJ} .
\label{eq:app-readout}
\end{equation}
A continuous-time comparator trips once, when the reference crosses
the lane, so the read is one noise sample per lane, which is what the
emulator computes, provided the reference crosses the comparator's
$\pm 3\sigma$ band within one noise correlation time: a slope of at
least $6 v_n \cdot 2\pi B$, or 38\,mV/ns at $v_n = 2$\,mV and
$B = 500$\,MHz, which sweeps the 20\,mV range in about half a
nanosecond. Its noise is set by its bias, since the input-referred
thermal noise is $4 \kB T \gamma B / g_m$ with $g_m = I / (n V_T)$,
which is the starved-preamplifier mechanism of
Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator}; at a fixed bandwidth-time product the
energy a read needs to reach a noise $v_n$ is $4 \kB T \gamma\, n V_T
V_{dd}\, (BT) / v_n^2$, with $\gamma = 1$, $n V_T = 35$\,mV and $BT =
3$: 0.35\,fJ at the 2\,mV hot read, 1.4\,fJ at 1\,mV (register code
45), 15\,fJ at 300\,$\mu$V (code 10) and 139\,fJ at 100\,$\mu$V
(code 0). The cold reads are 64\% of the reads, so a cold read at code
45 costs what is priced and one at code 0 costs ten times more; the
control of Sec.~\ref{sec:comparator} found code 45 moves no judge.
Table~\ref{tab:readout} prices the alternatives, printed by
\texttt{experiments/38\_readout\_options.py}. The choice is one of
generality against specificity: a falling reference read by a clocked
comparator returns the rank of every lane but pays a strobe, and a
fresh noise sample, per reference step; a pairwise tournament of
clocked decisions returns the winner alone, which is all a generation
uses, at one decision per lane; the continuous-time comparator returns
both for a standing bias whose cost is set by the noise demanded of
it.

\emph{Rails.} $V_a$ and $V_b$ are asserted for the read pulse at the
voltage the instruction carries and sit at the node potential between
reads, so a lane conducts only during its own read; a rail held across
the image would have every selected device in the bank conducting
throughout. Charging the rails per read is bounded in
Sec.~\ref{app:excluded} and carried as zero.

\subsection{Sneak paths and the ways out}\label{app:sneak}

A floating line is not an open circuit: it settles where its net
current is zero and passes current from one device to the next. With
every device of a $4 \times 4$ array at a common conductance $G$, the
sneak network between the selected row and the selected column is
three lumped stages of $3G$, $9G$ and $3G$ in series, $1.29G$ in
parallel with the selected device, so the selected device is $7/16$ of
the conductance the node sees. To first order the read of a symbol is
44\% that symbol, 48\% the six symbols that share its row or column of
the array, and 8\% the other nine; a feedback pulse divides the same
way, so those six see $3/7$ of every pulse. At the $16 \times 16$
corner the selected share is 12\%. The nodal solve behind these
numbers is \texttt{experiments/36\_sneak\_paths.py}.

Table~\ref{tab:sneak} prices three ways out on the same census and
constants. Only the node-side unselected lines need holding; the
rail-side lines can float once the node side is held. Holding them at
a fixed potential makes the differential read exact but leaves a shunt
of $2.25G$ per side on the node, a common gain loss of about three
that the hot read voltage must make up. Holding them at a replica of
$V_y$ is exact but needs a per-lane buffer that sinks three times the
selected pool's current, and the model cannot price its bias without
the device conductance it was built to cancel. A $16 \times 1$
selector column, one 1-of-16 multiplexer on the rail side and no
multiplexer on the node, has no second line for current to sneak onto
and removes the problem in the geometry: a 16-line decoder is little
more than two 4-line decoders, and because one select line is charged
per assertion instead of two it prices below the $4 \times 4$ under
either layout of its pass gates. It costs twice the select lines to
route and, with fifteen off gates on the rail-side lines of each side,
two and a half times the leakage paths, which stay negligible only
because the rails are pulsed per read (Sec.~\ref{app:excluded}); held
rails would leak a quarter to a half of its total.

\subsection{Bounds on the excluded terms}\label{app:excluded}

\emph{Rails.} The rails run along each lane beside $V_y$; their wire
capacitance across all 10\,080 lanes is 1.4\,nF. Asserting a bank's
rails once per read step at that read's voltage, 128 steps for the
$g$ bank and one for each of the others, costs $5 \times
10^{-12}$\,J per image, 0.3\% of the nominal total.

\emph{Leakage.} No transistor sits at a memristor, and the select lines
have no DC path. The DC paths are the off pass gates of both
multiplexers of a pair while its rail is asserted: $2 \times 2 \times
3 = 12$ per space, $2.1 \times 10^{7}$ over the census, at about 5\,pA
each with at most the read voltage across them. Because the rails are
asserted per read, those gates leak only during the read pulses: for
pulses of 10\,ns per step that is $2.5 \times 10^{-13}$\,J per image,
below $10^{-4}$ of the total. Had the rails been held across an assumed
30\,$\mu$s image at 50\,mV, the same gates would leak $1.6 \times
10^{-10}$\,J, 8\% of the total, which is one reason the rails are
pulsed. The image time itself depends on the device's conductance,
which the model does not need and the paper does not claim.

\emph{Comparator kickback.} A strobe injects about 0.5\,fC through the
input pair; on a node of at least 131\,fF that is 4\,mV, repeatable
strobe to strobe and so removed by the same per-lane trim as the
comparator's offset, and 25 times below the device's
0.1\,V erase threshold.

\emph{Read disturb.} The cold read at 50\,mV is two times below the
erase threshold and five times below the 0.25\,V write threshold; the
hot read at 10\,mV is ten and twenty-five times below.

\subsection{Basis of comparison}\label{app:basis}

Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm} models its per-sample energy as $E = T
K_{\mathrm{mix}} L^2 E_{\mathrm{cell}}$ with $E_{\mathrm{cell}} =
E_{\mathrm{rng}} + E_{\mathrm{bias}} + E_{\mathrm{comm}} +
E_{\mathrm{clock}} \approx 2$\,fJ per conditional update, the RNG term
measured on a test chip and the rest from foundry models at a node the
paper does not name. It excludes weight programming, host
communication, and die area. The present model counts the same kinds of
term (wire charging, per-cell readout, the analog operation itself) for
the same unit of work (one generated image), excludes the same things,
and states its process class explicitly. One difference runs against
this model: Ref.~\cite{jelincic2025dtm} signals neighbours at $4 V_T
\approx 100$\,mV and clocks at $5 V_T$, while the select lines here
are charged at the 0.8\,V core supply; the 0.5\,V row shows what a
lower swing returns. The chain energy used throughout,
$1.57\times10^{-8}$\,J, is the deepest point of their Fig.~1 decoded
from the vector figure ($1.961\,T$\,nJ at $T = 8$); their text puts
the same model at ``around $1.6\,T$\,nJ,'' which is
$1.28\times10^{-8}$\,J and makes the nominal ratio 6.4 rather than
7.9. A reader who does not grant the lane decode of
Sec.~\ref{sec:energy} should carry a digital $784 \times 226$
matrix-vector product instead, 1.8--8.9\,nJ at 10--50\,fJ per 8-bit
multiply-accumulate on the 2.0\,nJ total. The two accountings are
therefore comparable in kind, and both
are estimates: theirs extrapolated from one fabricated circuit, ours
from a stated geometry.
